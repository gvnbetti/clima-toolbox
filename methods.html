Methods

Background

Buildings have always been about protecting people and adapting to climate. Understanding the relationship between a building and its climate, a city and its climate, and ultimately anything we build and the climate it stands in, should be one of the foundations of design. Not the only one, but a fundamental one. It matters even more now: the energy used to heat and cool buildings is a major driver of global emissions, while the climate in which buildings stand is changing.

In practice, climate-adaptive and bioclimatic design are still often treated as specialist tasks, requiring specialised software and knowledge.

CBE Clima (Betti et al., 2024) was born from the idea that climate data should be easy to access and easy to understand, without specialised tools or expertise. The aim was to lower the barrier to entry. Clima Toolbox follows the same ethos, builds on that experience, and takes it a step further.

Clima Map and Macro Clima extend what CBE Clima set out to do: make weather files accessible and climate data easy to read. Three things are built into them from the start:

  • Future climate. Every weather file can be explored under +1.5, +2 and +3 °C of global warming.
  • Comparison. Every climate reading can be understood in relation to other climates.
  • The city. Weather files are usually recorded at airports or in open country, while many of the buildings we design stand in cities. Urban modifiers bring the climate data closer to the actual conditions of the place being designed.

Micro Clima and Room Clima take this a step further. They work as design recommendation systems, running thousands of what-if scenarios to identify which combinations of a few key parameters can create more comfortable outdoor and indoor environments.

Making something easy to access does not mean making it simpler than it needs to be. A considerable amount of mathematics runs underneath these apps, and we try to be as transparent about it as possible. That is the purpose of the methods and validation pages. Some assumptions are self-evident; others are there for expert users to question and challenge. All the tools are free to use and free to inspect, in the hope that others will help us make them better.

The five apps

Clima Map finds the most appropriate weather file for a given location.

Macro Clima helps understand the climate: comfort, sun, wind, sky and rain, and the design strategies that respond to them. It explores present conditions, future warming and the influence of the city.

Micro Clima explores how design choices shape the outdoor environment, testing the effects of shade, ground materials, wind protection and enclosure.

Room Clima explores the relationship between a room and its facade, testing windows, glazing, shading, thermal mass and ventilation, and their effects on energy use, comfort, daylight and carbon.

Clima Morph writes the future and urban climate into the weather file itself, so that the same conditions can be carried into other simulation tools.

Principles

Designed for the early stages

The apps focus on the information available at the beginning of a project: a location, a type of space, a few dimensions and a set of design options. When details are not yet known, they use clearly stated typical values rather than requiring information that may not exist yet.

Explore alternatives, not predict outcomes

The apps compare design options under the same climate conditions and assumptions. The differences between alternatives are generally more reliable than any single absolute result. The tools are designed primarily to help understand these differences and support informed design decisions.

Performance against fixed targets

Results are measured against fixed thresholds drawn from established standards and guidance, such as comfort ranges, overheating limits and daylight requirements. They are never measured against the best-performing option in a particular run. If a design does not meet a target, it is shown as such, regardless of how it compares with the alternatives.

Open about assumptions

Every assumption is documented, with its unit, source and status: standard, secondary source, design decision or assumption. Room Clima brings these into a single register, while Macro Clima and Micro Clima document them in their respective guides. Placeholder values are explicitly identified.

Established models, independently checked

The apps build on published scientific models and methods, including EN ISO 52016-1 (ISO, 2017), Perez (Perez et al., 1990), UTCI (Bröde et al., 2012), Theeuwes (Theeuwes et al., 2017), the IPCC AR6 Atlas (Gutiérrez et al., 2021) and Radiance's sky model (Perez et al., 1993; Ward, 1994). Their results are checked against established simulation tools used in the field, including EnergyPlus (Crawley et al., 2001), Radiance (Ward, 1994), pvlib (Holmgren et al., 2018), pythermalcomfort (Tartarini & Schiavon, 2020) and Ladybug (Sadeghipour Roudsari & Pak, 2013). These comparisons are model-to-model validations, rather than comparisons with measured building performance. See Validation for details.

Everything runs in the browser

There is nothing to install, and weather files remain on the user's computer. All calculations run directly in the browser. The underlying models are kept compact and transparent, so that their assumptions and methods can be inspected.
