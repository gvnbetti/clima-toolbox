Room Clima: method

What it is for

Exploring and comparing room and facade designs at concept stage.

The user defines a room type (dwelling, office or classroom), its dimensions, orientation and position in the building (single facade, corner or corner beneath the roof), and the design options to compare.

These include window size, glazing, fixed shading, blinds, thermal mass, insulation, natural ventilation, ceiling fans and, where needed, a chilled ceiling.

For each combination, Room Clima calculates overheating, delivered energy, daylight and whole-life carbon. It evaluates the results against fixed performance targets, ranks the designs and tests the most promising options under future warming and less favourable operating conditions.

The result is a shortlist of facade and room configurations that meet the requirements, together with an explanation of why they perform as they do.

A transparent register of assumptions. Every constant used by the app is defined in one place, room/src/register.js, with its unit, source and status. Each entry is classified as one of four types:

  • Standard: taken directly from the text of the named standard.
  • Secondary: taken from a secondary source, pending replacement with the original standard text.
  • Decision: a documented modelling choice made specifically for this app, with its date.
  • Assumption: a typical value without a binding source.

The complete register is available under "Method and limits" in the app. This page explains how the different calculation modules work together.

Thermal model

The thermal engine calculates the room's hourly heat balance, taking account of solar gains, internal loads, heat transfer through the building elements, ventilation and heating or cooling systems.

Comfort and the German summer check

Room Clima evaluates thermal comfort using several approaches. This allows the results to be interpreted against international comfort standards as well as the specific requirements of German building regulations.

Model Comfort band Status
EN 16798-1 adaptive (CEN, 2019) t = 0.33 trm + 18.8, categories I to III, using an exponential running mean with alpha 0.8. Secondary
ASHRAE 55 adaptive (ASHRAE, 2020b; de Dear & Brager, 1998) t = 0.31 tpma + 17.8, with 80% and 90% acceptability, using a seven-day prevailing mean. Secondary
DIN EN 16798-1/NA, Germany (DIN, 2021) 22 to 26 °C, with a slope of 0.25 between outdoor temperatures of 16 and 32 °C and category-dependent tolerance. Secondary
Cooled rooms Fixed upper comfort limits based on EN 16798-1 design values, with ASHRAE 55 limits following an app-specific modelling decision. Secondary and assumption

Overheating is measured as degree-hours above the applicable comfort band during occupied hours. Up to 5% of occupied hours above the band is accepted without cooling, following the informative guidance in EN 15251 (CEN, 2007).

For Germany, Room Clima also performs the summer overheating assessment according to DIN 4108-2 (DIN, 2013). This calculation uses the standard's prescribed conditions, including schedules, internal gains, air change rates, blind operation, reference temperatures for summer climate regions A, B and C, and the applicable limits of 1,200 or 500 K·h/a.

When the assessment uses a weather file other than the official test reference year for the relevant region (Bundesinstitut für Bau-, Stadt- und Raumforschung, 2017), the result is an estimate rather than a formal compliance assessment. The app identifies this distinction explicitly.

The text of DIN 4108-2 is not included in this repository.

Daylight

Room Clima calculates climate-based daylight in the room directly in the browser. The implementation is in the first part of room/src/core.js.

The method combines a radiosity solution for the six room surfaces with hourly sky conditions, direct solar radiation and glazing properties.

Sky and daylight coefficients. Daylight coefficients are calculated using a radiosity solution of the six room surfaces. The sky and ground are represented by Reinhart sky patches (MF:2), following the approach used by Radiance (Bourgeois et al., 2008; Tregenza, 1987).

Sky conditions. The sky is recalculated for each hour using the Perez all-weather sky model (Perez et al., 1993) and the luminous efficacy model of Perez et al. (1990). The implementation reproduces the approach of Radiance's gendaymtx (Ward, 1994); the sun position comes from the shared calculation core, as in the thermal model.

Direct sunlight. Direct solar radiation is calculated using an exact ray test from each sensor point through the glazing. Glazing transmission follows the Radiance glass model (Larson & Shakespeare, 1998).

Blinds. Venetian blinds are represented as a two-part optical layer, following a modelling decision dated 20 November 2026. A small proportion of light passes directly through, while the remainder is scattered evenly.

  • Exterior blinds: total transmission 0.10, of which 0.01 passes directly through.
  • Interior blinds: total transmission 0.25, of which 0.03 passes directly through, with a reflectance of 0.6.

The angular behaviour of individual slats is not modelled.

Useful daylight. Useful daylight (Nabil & Mardaljevic, 2005) is expressed as the share of floor area and occupied hours within the following illuminance ranges:

  • Non-residential: 300 to 3,000 lx.
  • Residential: 100 to 2,000 lx.

The corresponding EN 17037 daylight levels (CEN, 2018) are also reported.

Electric lighting. Where daylight falls below the target illuminance, supplementary LED lighting is estimated at 6 W/m², providing 500 lx in offices and 300 lx in classrooms.

Validation. The daylight calculation has been checked against Radiance using six room configurations, six climates and three blind conditions. Results are documented in validation/daylight/.

Scores, recommendations and search

Room Clima evaluates design alternatives against fixed performance targets. Scores are measured against these targets, never relative to the best or worst option in a particular run.

Performance scores.

  • Daylight: based on the share of useful daylight within the specified illuminance range.
  • Comfort: scores 1 when there is no overheating and 0.5 at the overheating limit.
  • Energy: scores 1 at or below the target and 0.5 at the performance limit.

Recommendation. Among designs that meet every target, Room Clima recommends the option with the highest overall score. Where scores differ by no more than 0.01, the design with lower whole-life carbon takes priority.

If no design meets all targets, the app identifies the closest alternative based on its largest weighted performance shortfall.

Two requirements are treated as non-negotiable: the minimum daylight requirement and the DIN 4108-2 summer overheating assessment cannot be traded against other performance criteria.

Design packages. For each facade, the app can propose up to five additional design packages. These differ from the recommended option in at least two design measures, allowing users to explore meaningful alternatives rather than minor variations.

Search strategy. The design space is searched using pruning to eliminate combinations that cannot meet the requirements. A subsequent local search identifies the lowest-carbon design among those meeting all targets.

During development, this search strategy was compared with a full enumeration of the available options. That benchmark is not yet included in the repository.

Scenarios

The recommended design and its alternative packages are tested under two additional conditions.

Future climate. The designs are evaluated under +2 °C global warming, or +3 °C when the original run already uses +2 °C. The weather-file adjustment follows the shared future-climate method.

Poor operation. The app tests a less favourable pattern of occupant behaviour: blinds are lowered only every other day, and windows remain closed at night.

Robustness. A design is considered robust when it continues to meet the comfort limit under these scenarios, or remains the best-performing option within the shortlist.

Carbon

Whole-life carbon is calculated separately from the thermal and daylight models, using the materials, quantities, replacement cycles and operational energy associated with each design.

See the dedicated Room Clima carbon method for the system boundaries, data sources, calculation approach and assumptions.

Assumptions

A single, empty room. The model represents one rectangular room without internal partitions or furniture. Floors, ceilings and internal partitions are cut at their mid-plane and do not exchange heat with adjacent rooms. Neighbouring spaces are assumed to be at the same temperature.

Fixed occupant behaviour. Windows, blinds, ceiling fans and night ventilation operate according to predefined thresholds. These rules apply consistently across the simulations. The poor-operation scenario tests the sensitivity of the results to less favourable behaviour.

Conservative natural ventilation. Window ventilation is driven by temperature differences only. Wind could increase airflow, but is not included because reliable site-specific wind conditions are not available at concept stage.

Ideal heating and cooling. Heating and cooling are assumed to be delivered ideally through all-electric heat pumps. Delivered energy and associated operational carbon are calculated from the corresponding system efficiencies.

Limits

Thermal model accuracy. The settings of the hourly model were chosen on Room Clima's own rooms in the six bundled climates and tested again in eight climates not used for that choice. In those rooms, hourly results agree with EnergyPlus within 0.69 K and 0.75 K (RMSE), about 0.5 K on the cool side, and the two rank designs alike. In the ASHRAE Standard 140 test box, the model falls within 32 of the 44 reported ranges and overestimates cooling by 12.7 %.

Blinds. Venetian blinds are represented as a diffusing optical layer in the daylight calculation. The angle-dependent behaviour of individual slats is not modelled.

German summer assessment. The DIN 4108-2 assessment is an estimate when performed using a weather file other than the official test reference year for the relevant climate region.

Glare and views. The daylight assessment does not evaluate visual glare, view quality or the relationship between daylight and the outside view.

Validation

The calculation modules have been checked against established simulation tools, reference methods and test cases.
