Micro Clima: method

What it is for

Testing design choices for an outdoor space: a square, a courtyard, a street or a terrace.

Micro Clima explores how the design of a space changes the thermal conditions people experience within it. It calculates hours of heat and cold stress at a reference point in the centre of the space, using UTCI, and compares different design options: trees or a canopy, paving or grass, an open space or an enclosed courtyard, more or less wind shelter.

The weather file describes the climate at an open site. Micro Clima models how the geometry, materials, vegetation and shelter of the designed space modify those conditions.

How it works

The calculation engine is implemented in the script within micro/index.html. It evaluates the outdoor environment hour by hour across the weather file, combining surface heat balances, solar radiation, vegetation, geometry and wind exposure to calculate thermal comfort. Sun position, solar radiation on walls, saturation vapour pressure, SolarCal, UTCI, future climate and the urban heat island come from the calculation core shared with Macro Clima and Room Clima (shared/climamods.js).

Part Method Source
Neighbourhood air temperature Weather-file temperature adjusted for the urban heat island. Additional daytime cooling from tree canopy is applied only above 40% canopy cover; its strength is currently a placeholder. Theeuwes et al. (2017); Ziter et al. (2019)
Ground surface One-dimensional implicit heat balance extending to 4 m depth, accounting for solar radiation, longwave exchange, convection and evaporation according to surface moisture availability. Oke (1987); material properties documented in the guide, "Where the values come from"
Walls Separate surface heat balances for each orientation, using a fixed indoor temperature. Surface heat balance model
Mean radiant temperature Longwave radiation exchange calculated using view factors for the sky, ground, canopy and walls, combined with SolarCal for direct and diffuse solar exposure of the body, in standing or seated positions. Arens et al. (2015)
Trees Big-leaf energy balance model of the tree canopy, including solar transmission for leaf-on and leaf-off conditions. Campbell & Norman (1998); Leuning (1995); further sources in the guide
Built canopy Thin-sheet heat balance, using a white membrane with 10% solar transmission and 15% absorption. Material assumptions documented in the guide
Enclosures Four spatial configurations: open field, single wall, courtyard and street canyon, with height-to-width ratios (H/W) of 0.5, 1 and 2. Sky view factors and solar access are calculated geometrically at the centre point. Geometric calculations
Wind Weather-station wind speed at 10 m, adjusted using wind shelter and enclosure factors. These factors are currently placeholders. Assumptions documented in the guide
Outdoor comfort UTCI calculated using the operational polynomial and its associated thermal stress categories. Bröde et al. (2012)
Future and urban climate Adjustments for global warming scenarios and urban heat island effects. See Future and urban climate. See dedicated method

Assumptions

One reference point. The model evaluates conditions at a single point in the centre of the space. Conditions near walls, at the edges of tree canopies or in other parts of the space may be different. The results therefore represent the conditions at this reference point, not a spatial average.

Solar exposure and shading. Solar position and sky exposure are calculated geometrically for the centre point. However, walls do not shade one another in the surface heat balance: each wall is modelled as though it were exposed to the sun independently.

Parameters still under development. Some parameters are currently represented by placeholders rather than established empirical values. These include wind shelter factors, enclosure-related wind adjustments, the strength of canopy cooling and the moisture availability of permeable paving. They are explicitly identified in the guide.

Limits

Wind. Wind speed is adjusted using shelter and enclosure factors, rather than calculated through computational fluid dynamics (CFD). Gusts, corner effects, local wind acceleration and channelled flows are therefore not represented.

Geometry. The model uses a fixed set of enclosure configurations. A real outdoor space is approximated by the closest available geometry, rather than modelled in all its architectural detail.

Validation

The main calculation components have been checked against established reference implementations and analytical solutions.

UTCI matches pythermalcomfort (Tartarini & Schiavon, 2020). SolarCal (Arens et al., 2015) matches it within 0.06 K; the difference comes from the radiation heat transfer coefficient, 6.012 W/m²K as in ASHRAE 55 (ASHRAE, 2020b) and Ladybug, which pythermalcomfort rounds to 6. Solar position agrees with pvlib (Holmgren et al., 2018) within 0.02°. Ground and wall heat balances have been checked against exact solutions (Carslaw & Jaeger, 1959).

The overall performance ranking of 70 design configurations has also been compared with Ladybug Tools (Sadeghipour Roudsari & Pak, 2013), achieving a rank correlation of 0.97.

These checks assess the consistency of the calculations and the relative ranking of design alternatives. They do not constitute validation against measured conditions in real outdoor spaces. See outdoor comfort.
