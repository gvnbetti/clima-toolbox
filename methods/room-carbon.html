Room Clima: how carbon is calculated

What it measures

Room Clima compares design options by their whole-life carbon over a 50-year study period, expressed per m² of floor area.

It counts only the impacts that change between the options: the embodied carbon of elements added or removed, including their replacements, and the operational carbon associated with the energy used by the room.

The structure, floors, partitions and building services shared by all options are excluded. The result is therefore a comparative assessment of alternative designs for the same room, not a whole-building LCA.

Code: carbonOf in room/src/core.js. Data: the carbon.* entries in room/src/register.js.

All embodied-carbon values are generic assumptions. They should be replaced with project-specific data or, where available, product and assembly data from the Build-up LCA datasets, including ÖKOBAUDAT (Bundesministerium für Wohnen, Stadtentwicklung und Bauwesen, n.d.-b).

Calculation

Whole-life carbon = embodied carbon of the design options + operational carbon over 50 years

The study period is 50 years, following the reference study period used by BNB (Bundesministerium für Wohnen, Stadtentwicklung und Bauwesen, n.d.-a) and DGNB (Deutsche Gesellschaft für Nachhaltiges Bauen, n.d.).

Replacement cycles. An element with a service life of L years is counted according to the number of times it is installed during the study period: ⌈50 / L⌉.

Operational carbon. Delivered energy is multiplied by an assumed electricity-grid factor of 0.25 kg CO₂e/kWh and by the 50-year study period.

The factor represents an assumed average over the study period, not today's grid intensity. Germany's electricity mix was approximately 0.41 kg CO₂e/kWh in 2024, including upstream emissions (Icha & Lauf, 2026). The lower factor reflects the expected decarbonisation of electricity generation. It can be changed in the app.

Window area is calculated from the window-to-wall ratio and facade area. A corner room includes both facades. For overhangs, the projected area is depth multiplied by length; for fins, it is their projection multiplied by height.

The thermal-mass values are the least certain in this dataset and should be treated as indicative.

Worked example

An office room with one facade, measuring 3.6 × 5.4 m and 3.0 m high. This gives 19.44 m² of floor area and 10.8 m² of facade area.

Operational carbon adds 12.5 kg CO₂e/m² for every 1 kWh/m²a of delivered energy: 0.25 × 50.

At 10 kWh/m²a, this amounts to 125 kg CO₂e/m² over 50 years, comparable in magnitude to the embodied carbon of the options. A design that saves 2 kWh/m²a therefore avoids approximately 25 kg CO₂e/m² over the study period. This provides a useful way to compare additional embodied carbon against operational energy savings.

How carbon influences the recommendation

Carbon is not a performance target and does not contribute to the design score. It is used to distinguish between designs with otherwise equivalent performance.

Within the same tier, when scores are within 0.01 of each other, the design with lower whole-life carbon is preferred.

A further local search looks for the lowest-carbon design among those meeting every target. This is necessary because carbon is not one of the main search criteria, and the initial search could otherwise miss a lower-carbon option.

The recommendation therefore prioritises meeting the comfort, energy and daylight requirements, then uses whole-life carbon to identify the better-performing option among eligible designs.
