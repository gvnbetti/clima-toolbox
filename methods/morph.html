Clima Morph: method

What it is for

Carrying the future and urban climate of the toolbox into other simulation tools.

Clima Morph applies the two climate modifiers shared by Macro, Micro and Room Clima to a weather file, and returns a complete EPW file that EnergyPlus, Ladybug, Honeybee and other programs can read. It adds no model of its own: a file morphed here, read back in Macro Clima as recorded, gives the same hours as Macro Clima's own future and urban settings, to within the file's rounding.

How it works

Reading the file. The weather file is read with Macro Clima's reader. Hourly EPW files are accepted, or a ZIP holding one, as downloaded from Clima Map. The file stays in the browser.

Changing the weather. The hourly values come from the shared calculation core (shared/climamods.js): first the future climate, then the urban heat island on top, as described in Future and urban climate. Under the future climate, relative humidity is held and dew point recomputed (Belcher et al., 2005). Under the heat island, dew point is held and relative humidity recomputed (Theeuwes et al., 2017).

Writing the file. The source file is copied and only these fields are rewritten (EPW field numbers as in the EnergyPlus documentation; U.S. Department of Energy, 2024):

Field Variable Changed by Precision
7 dry-bulb temperature future climate and heat island 0.1 °C
8 dew point future climate 0.1 °C
9 relative humidity heat island 1 %
13 horizontal infrared radiation from the sky future climate 1 W/m²
34 precipitation depth future climate as the source, at least 0.1 mm

Solar radiation, wind, pressure, cloud and dates are copied unchanged. A value missing in the source stays missing. A dew point never lies above the dry-bulb temperature after rounding.

The header. The design conditions describe the present climate, so they are removed; the line stays as DESIGN CONDITIONS,0, which keeps the eight header lines every program expects. The ground temperatures are raised by the annual mean future warming; the heat island does not change them. The typical and extreme periods are kept as they were chosen on the present climate. The second comment line records what was done: the toolbox version, the warming level, the IPCC region, the file's years, the neighbourhood and the fields changed and kept. It is written without commas, since some programs split comment lines at commas. The file name records the settings, for example …_+2C_urban.epw.

Assumptions

In a leap-year file, the 24 hours of 29 February take the hourly changes of 28 February, since the core works on 365 days. Files with more than one record per hour are not supported.

Limits

The limits of the two modifiers apply to the file: the sequence of weather is that of the recorded years, so future heat waves are not made longer or stronger; the heat island is a neighbourhood average calibrated in north-western Europe; sun, wind and cloud are not changed. Sky infrared under the future climate is scaled with air temperature alone; a rise in sky emissivity with the moister air (Clark & Allen, 1978) is planned in the shared core.

Validation

The file read back matches the core within its rounding in every hour, unchanged fields are copied byte for byte, and EnergyPlus and Ladybug read every morphed file: see Clima Morph.
